Section 1 · Key concepts

Skills gap vs. skills mismatch

Skills gap

The difference between the competencies employers require and the competencies workers or applicants currently possess.

Skills mismatch

A situation where a worker's qualifications, field of training, or actual skills do not correspond to the requirements of the job.

Five ways a mismatch shows up

Types of skills gaps and mismatches

TypeMeaningExample
Skills shortageToo few workers possess a needed skill.Not enough qualified automation technicians.
Skills deficitWorkers lack a specific competency.A technician cannot troubleshoot PLC systems.
Skills obsolescencePreviously useful skills become outdated.A worker is unfamiliar with modern digital tools.
Horizontal mismatchField of training differs from the occupation.A graduate works in an unrelated job.
Vertical mismatchQualification level differs from job requirements.A worker is overqualified or underqualified.

Section 2 · Root causes

Why skills gaps exist

Rapid technological change — automation, AI, cloud, robotics, renewable energy.

Weak education–industry connections; programs reflect tradition over demand.

Outdated facilities and equipment, unlike real workplace tools.

Insufficient practical exposure — little speed, judgment, or teamwork building.

Weak foundational and soft skills: communication, digital literacy, professionalism.

Unequal access to training for rural, low-income, and informal workers.

Section 3 · The response

Major roles of TVE

Develop job-specific competencies

Electrical, welding, automotive, ICT, hospitality, construction, agriculture, health, manufacturing.

Translate labor-market needs

Uses vacancies, employer surveys, forecasts, and graduate data to spot needed competencies.

Provide competency-based training

Focuses on what learners can demonstrate safely and accurately — not just recall.

Support reskilling & upskilling

Prepares workers for new occupations and helps them adapt to technological change.

Connect learners with workplaces

Apprenticeships, internships, dual and enterprise-based training, workplace simulations.

Improve employability

Builds technical, digital, interpersonal, problem-solving, safety, and entrepreneurial skills.

Promote inclusion — expanding opportunities for unemployed adults, out-of-school youth, women, persons with disabilities, rural and displaced workers.

Section 4 · Framework

How TVE addresses a skills gap

Eight steps, applied continuously — identifying, translating, delivering, and revising.

Steps 1–2 of 8

1. Identify the gap

Through labor-market information and employer consultation.

2. Convert into competencies

Turn the gap into measurable competencies and learning outcomes.

Steps 3–4 of 8

3. Update the program

Curriculum, equipment, software, and learning resources.

4. Prepare instructors

Through industry immersion and professional development.

Steps 5–6 of 8

5. Provide realistic tasks

Project-based, problem-based, and enterprise-based learning.

6. Assess authentic performance

Demonstrations, portfolios, workplace observation, practical exams.

Steps 7–8 of 8

7. Track outcomes

Employment, job relevance, retention, productivity, employer satisfaction.

8. Revise continuously

Use feedback to keep improving the program — feeding back into Step 1.

Section 6 · What's in the way

Challenges facing TVE

  • Limited funding for modern equipment and laboratories.
  • Shortage of trainers with current industry experience.
  • Slow curriculum updating and insufficient labor-market data.
  • Weak employer participation and limited workplace training slots.
  • Unequal access to certification and quality training.
  • Insufficient graduate tracking and employer feedback.
  • Differences between national priorities and local employment opportunities.

Section 7 · Moving forward

Strategies for stronger TVE

Institutions

Establish industry advisory boards, update programs with labor-market data, modernize facilities, provide instructor immersion, expand workplace learning, use authentic assessment, track graduates.

Industry

Participate in curriculum review, provide trainers and assessors, offer apprenticeships, share current standards, evaluate student performance, give feedback.

Government

Improve labor-market information, fund facilities and trainer upgrading, incentivize enterprise-based training, coordinate TESDA, schools, employers, and LGUs.
