Defining the concept

What is industry alignment?

Industry alignment is the process of ensuring a TVE program develops the knowledge, skills, attitudes, and work habits required in actual workplaces.

A program is aligned when graduates can transfer what they learned in school to actual work situations. In the Philippine context, training–job mismatch can occur when course offerings, curricula, equipment, and training methods fail to keep pace with employer demand — which is why industry participation matters across curriculum development, training delivery, assessment, and certification.

Why it matters

Without alignment, graduates hold certificates — not capability

Outdated tools, machines, software, and equipment.

Training content that no longer reflects current workplace procedures.

Too much theory, not enough hands-on practice.

Inadequate workplace exposure.

Assessments that test memory instead of actual performance.

Weak communication, teamwork, problem-solving, and digital skills.

Policy context

TESDA's push for stronger industry involvement

TESDA has emphasized deeper industry involvement in competency standards, training regulations, enterprise-based training, and assessment.

Enterprise-based training

Learners study institutional concepts while applying them directly in a company setting — putting the workplace inside the curriculum, not just at the end of it.

Framework

The alignment cycle

Seven steps, continuously repeating — because alignment is never finished once the curriculum is written.

Step 1 of 7

Identify industry demand

Alignment starts by gathering real signal from the labor market, not assumptions.

  • Local employers and industry associations
  • Job advertisements and labor-market studies
  • Professional organizations and government employment data
  • Alumni and graduate employment records
  • Industry advisory boards

Step 2 of 7

Translate demand into competencies

Industry needs must become clear, observable learning outcomes — not vague statements like "understand technology."

Industry requirementTVE competency
Operate computerized equipmentSet up, operate, and troubleshoot equipment safely
Prepare digital reportsCreate accurate reports using appropriate software
Follow workplace safety rulesApply occupational safety and health procedures
Work with clientsDemonstrate professional communication and customer service
Solve equipment problemsDiagnose faults and apply corrective procedures

Step 3 of 7

Align the curriculum

The curriculum is built around units of competency, shaped directly by industry input.

  • Current occupational standards
  • Industry processes and workflows
  • Required technical skills and workplace safety requirements
  • Digital and technological developments
  • Professional and ethical expectations
  • Local and regional employment opportunities

Step 4 of 7

Align learning resources and facilities

A program can't claim to be industry-aligned if learners train only on obsolete or unavailable equipment.

  • Machines, tools, and consumable materials
  • Software and digital platforms
  • Workshop layout and simulation facilities
  • Safety devices and internet connectivity
  • Maintenance and replacement schedules

Step 5 of 7

Align teaching and learning activities

Teaching should reflect how work is actually performed in industry.

  • Demonstration and return demonstration
  • Project- and problem-based learning
  • Simulated and production-based workplace tasks
  • Industry visits and guest lectures by practitioners
  • Apprenticeships, internships, and enterprise-based training

Every activity should answer one question: what workplace task will the student be able to perform after this?

Step 6 of 7

Align assessment

Assessment should measure authentic performance, not only written knowledge.

  • Practical demonstrations and performance tasks
  • Portfolio assessment and product evaluation
  • Workplace observation and troubleshooting activities
  • Client-service simulations and oral questioning
  • Industry-based assessment

Step 7 of 7

Obtain employer feedback

Alignment isn't finished when the curriculum is written — the institution should keep asking:

  • Are our graduates employable, and performing the required tasks?
  • Which skills are still weak?
  • What technologies or procedures have changed?
  • Which competencies should be added, revised, or removed?

This creates a feedback loop between school and industry — which feeds straight back into Step 1.

“

An industry-aligned TVE program is not simply a program that offers employment-related subjects. It is a continuously updated system in which industry helps define the competencies, learning experiences, equipment, assessment methods, and graduate outcomes.
